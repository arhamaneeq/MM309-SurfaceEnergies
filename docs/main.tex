\begin{abstract}
Molecular-dynamics simulations were used to investigate potential-energy-based surface-energy estimates for FCC-based Fe--Ni--Al alloys at 300, 500, and 700~K. Three alloy compositions, three crystallographic orientations, and three independent chemical configurations were examined using a modified embedded-atom method (MEAM) potential. Across the sampled conditions, the (111) surface consistently exhibited the lowest estimated surface energy, while (110) exhibited the highest. Fe$_{20}$Ni$_{50}$Al$_{30}$ showed the highest values overall, and Fe$_{50}$Ni$_{30}$Al$_{20}$ the lowest. Temperature dependence was weaker and less consistent than the effects of composition and orientation, with configuration-to-configuration variability limiting the interpretation of small changes. Preliminary slab-convergence tests suggested 12 atomic layers and 10~\AA{} of vacuum per side as provisional settings, but did not establish convergence for all production geometries. The results remain preliminary because the atom-type mapping discrepancy is unresolved and sampling is limited. Moreover, the calculated quantities are potential-energy-based estimates rather than thermodynamic surface free energies.
\end{abstract}
