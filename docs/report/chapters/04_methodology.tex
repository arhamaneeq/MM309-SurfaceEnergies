\section{Computational Methodology}

\subsection{Computational workflow and simulation environment}

The computational workflow couples Python-based structure generation with 
molecular-dynamics simulations performed using LAMMPS and a modified 
embedded-atom method (MEAM) interatomic potential. The workflow is organised 
into sequential stages: generation of bulk configurations, finite-temperature 
bulk equilibration, extraction of bulk reference properties, construction of 
surface slabs, generation of LAMMPS input decks, and execution of slab 
calculations. Structure metadata, simulation inputs, logs, and machine-readable 
summaries are retained to support reproducibility.

The structure-generation scripts use the Atomic Simulation Environment (ASE) 
together with project-specific Python modules. Bulk and slab structures are 
exported as LAMMPS data files. The MEAM parameter files \texttt{library.meam} 
and \texttt{FeNiAl.meam} are copied into the individual simulation directories, 
allowing each calculation to retain the potential files used to generate its 
results. The orchestration scripts also perform input preflight checks before 
launching pending simulations and use completion markers to distinguish jobs that 
have finished successfully from those requiring execution.

\subsection{Alloy compositions and chemical configurations}

Three nominal alloy compositions are considered:

\begin{align}
    \mathrm{FeNiAl}: \quad
    &x_{\mathrm{Fe}}=x_{\mathrm{Ni}}=x_{\mathrm{Al}}=\frac{1}{3},\\
    \mathrm{Fe}_{20}\mathrm{Ni}_{50}\mathrm{Al}_{30}: \quad
    &(x_{\mathrm{Fe}},x_{\mathrm{Ni}},x_{\mathrm{Al}})
      =(0.20,0.50,0.30),\\
    \mathrm{Fe}_{50}\mathrm{Ni}_{30}\mathrm{Al}_{20}: \quad
    &(x_{\mathrm{Fe}},x_{\mathrm{Ni}},x_{\mathrm{Al}})
      =(0.50,0.30,0.20),
\end{align}

where $x_i$ denotes the atomic fraction of element $i$. The equiatomic composition 
therefore contains approximately $33.33$ at.\% of each constituent.

Chemically disordered configurations are generated by assigning species to lattice 
sites according to the prescribed composition and a random seed. Three seeds, $20261024$, 
$20261025$, and $20261026$, are used to generate independent starting configurations. 
The bulk structure generator constructs $4\times4\times4$ supercells of the conventional 
FCC unit cell, corresponding to 256 lattice sites before any changes in atom count arising 
from the structure-generation implementation. An initial cubic lattice parameter of $3.60$~\AA\ 
is supplied as a trial value rather than assumed to be the equilibrium lattice parameter.

The nominal composition and realised atomic fractions are recorded for each generated structure. 
Because finite supercells contain integer numbers of atoms, the realised composition must be checked 
against the target fractions. The random seeds provide reproducible initial assignments, but three 
configurations do not constitute an exhaustive sampling of the configurational space of a chemically 
disordered alloy.

\subsection{Finite-temperature bulk equilibration}

Bulk reference calculations are performed separately for each alloy composition and target temperature 
of $300$, $500$, and $700$~K. The initial bulk structures are periodic in all three spatial directions. 
The bulk LAMMPS input decks employ metal units, an atomic representation, and the MEAM potential.

An initial conjugate-gradient energy minimisation is performed at the trial lattice parameter to reduce 
excessive initial forces and relax the atomic positions. The system is subsequently equilibrated using an 
isothermal--isobaric (NPT) ensemble at the target temperature and zero external pressure. Isotropic pressure 
control allows the periodic cell volume to respond to the imposed thermodynamic conditions. The timestep is 
$0.001$~ps, equivalent to $1$~fs. Each bulk calculation uses $5{,}000$ equilibration steps followed by 
$10{,}000$ production steps, corresponding to nominal durations of $5$ and $10$~ps, respectively.

During the production stage, the instantaneous potential energy, temperature, pressure, volume, and cell 
dimensions are recorded. The equivalent cubic FCC lattice parameter is calculated from the instantaneous 
volume per atom:

\begin{equation}
    a_{\mathrm{FCC}}(t)
    = \left(\frac{4V(t)}{N}\right)^{1/3},
    \label{eq:bulk-fcc-lattice}
\end{equation}

where $V(t)$ is the instantaneous volume and $N$ is the number of atoms. This relation assumes four atoms 
per conventional FCC unit cell and expresses the volume per atom as $a^3/4$. The mean lattice parameter and 
mean potential energy per atom are subsequently extracted for each alloy and temperature.

The resulting bulk summary provides a shared lattice-parameter estimate for each alloy--temperature combination. 
These values are used to generate temperature-specific bulk configurations for the surface calculations. The bulk 
reference energy per atom is likewise taken from the corresponding alloy--temperature entry. This matching prevents 
the reference energy from being transferred between different nominal compositions or temperatures.

The bulk simulations are intended to establish reference properties within the selected potential and simulation 
protocol. Their finite duration and the limited number of independent configurations must be considered when assessing 
statistical convergence. In particular, the equilibration and production lengths should not be assumed sufficient 
without examining the stability of the measured quantities.

\subsection{Surface slab construction}

Slabs are constructed from temperature-specific bulk configurations using the project structure-generation tools. The 
three crystallographic orientations investigated are FCC $(111)$, $(110)$, and $(100)$. For each alloy and target 
temperature, three chemically disordered parent configurations are generated using the prescribed seeds. Each parent 
configuration is used to construct slabs for all three orientations, preserving the correspondence between the bulk 
configuration and its associated surface models.

The slab generator uses $12$ atomic layers and introduces $10$~\AA\ of vacuum along the surface-normal direction. 
Periodic boundary conditions are retained in the two in-plane directions, while the surface-normal direction is 
non-periodic. The vacuum region separates periodic images of the slab and reduces spurious interactions between 
opposing surfaces under the selected potential and simulation settings.

For each slab, the generated metadata records the alloy, temperature, configuration identifier, parent random seed, 
lattice parameter, Miller indices, number of layers, vacuum thickness, surface area, and atomic thickness. These 
records provide a traceable link between each surface calculation and its parent configuration.

The resulting calculation matrix contains

\begin{equation}
    N_{\mathrm{slab}}
    = N_{\mathrm{alloy}}N_TN_{\mathrm{config}}N_{\mathrm{orientation}}
    = 3\times3\times3\times3
    = 81
    \label{eq:number-slab-calculations}
\end{equation}

slab jobs. This count describes the planned production matrix, not a claim that all calculations have completed 
successfully.

\subsection{Potential setup and atom-type assignment}

All simulations use the MEAM potential through the LAMMPS \texttt{pair\_style meam} implementation. The input decks 
assign atomic masses and map LAMMPS atom types to the chemical species represented by the potential. In the current 
production scripts, the intended mapping is type~1 = Al, type~2 = Fe, and type~3 = Ni. The \texttt{pair\_coeff} command 
specifies the elemental order in the MEAM library and the mapping from the LAMMPS atom types to the alloy elements.

This mapping must be consistent across the structure-generation routines, LAMMPS data-file \texttt{Masses} sections, 
and potential input commands. An inconsistent mapping can produce energies for an unintended chemical assignment without 
necessarily causing the simulation to terminate. The mapping should therefore be verified directly against the generated 
data files and input decks before production energies are accepted.

\subsection{Slab minimisation and molecular dynamics}

Each slab calculation begins with a conjugate-gradient energy minimisation while retaining the generated simulation-cell 
dimensions and vacuum separation. The minimisation criteria are an energy tolerance of $10^{-8}$ and a force tolerance of 
$10^{-6}$ in the units used by LAMMPS, with maximum limits of $2{,}000$ minimisation iterations and $20{,}000$ force 
evaluations. This step relaxes atomic positions without performing an independent cell-shape or cell-volume optimisation of 
the slab.

Following minimisation, atomic velocities are initialised at the target temperature using a Gaussian velocity distribution 
and a reproducible seed derived from the parent configuration, temperature, orientation, and alloy identifier. The slab is 
then equilibrated under a canonical (NVT) ensemble using a Nos\'e--Hoover thermostat. The simulation cell remains fixed, and 
the temperature is maintained at the target value.

The timestep is $1$~fs. The slab equilibration stage comprises $5{,}000$ steps ($5$~ps), followed by a production stage of 
$10{,}000$ steps ($10$~ps). The production run records the potential energy, temperature, and surface-energy estimate. Time 
averaging is performed every $1{,}000$ steps, using $100$ samples separated by $10$ timesteps for each output average.

These settings define the implemented simulation protocol. Whether the chosen durations provide adequate equilibration and 
statistical sampling must be assessed from the resulting time series and the variability between independent configurations.

\subsection{Surface-energy estimation}

The potential-energy-based surface-energy estimate is calculated using the slab energy and the matching bulk reference energy 
per atom:

\begin{equation}
    \gamma(t)
    = \frac{E_{\mathrm{slab}}(t)-N e_{\mathrm{bulk}}(T)}
           {2A},
    \label{eq:surface-energy}
\end{equation}

where $E_{\mathrm{slab}}(t)$ is the instantaneous total potential energy of the slab, $N$ is the number of slab atoms, 
$e_{\mathrm{bulk}}(T)$ is the mean bulk potential energy per atom associated with the same alloy and target temperature, 
and $A$ is the area of one exposed surface. The factor of two assumes that the slab contains two equivalent surfaces.

The energy difference in the numerator represents the excess potential energy relative to the bulk reference. When the energies 
are expressed in electronvolts and the surface area in \AA$^2$, the calculated value is converted to SI units using

\begin{equation}
    1~\mathrm{eV}/\AA^2
    = 16.02176634~\mathrm{J\,m^{-2}}.
\end{equation}

The LAMMPS input decks evaluate this estimate during the production trajectory and average it over the prescribed sampling 
interval.

The estimate depends on the consistency of the slab and bulk reference calculations. In particular, the bulk energy must correspond 
to the same nominal composition and target temperature as the slab. Differences in relaxation conventions, statistical sampling, or 
the thermodynamic states represented by the bulk and slab energies can introduce systematic errors into the excess-energy estimate.

At finite temperature, the time-averaged value of $\gamma(t)$ is not automatically the thermodynamic surface free energy. A free-energy 
calculation requires the relevant entropic contributions and a clearly defined thermodynamic reference. The quantities reported here 
should therefore be described as potential-energy-based surface-energy estimates under the specified simulation protocol.

\subsection{Configuration sampling and uncertainty}

For each alloy, temperature, and crystallographic orientation, three independent chemically disordered configurations are included in 
the planned production matrix. The configuration-specific surface-energy estimates can be used to assess the sensitivity of the result 
to the local atomic arrangement. For $n$ independent configurations, the arithmetic mean and sample standard deviation are

\begin{equation}
    \bar{\gamma}
    = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_i,
    \label{eq:surface-energy-mean}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    s_{\gamma}
    = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n-1}
    \sum_{i=1}^{n}(\gamma_i-\bar{\gamma})^2},
    \label{eq:surface-energy-std}
\end{equation}

where $\gamma_i$ is the configuration-level estimate obtained using a consistent averaging procedure. The sample standard deviation characterises 
the observed spread between configurations; it should not be interpreted as a complete uncertainty estimate because it does not automatically 
include systematic potential errors, finite-size effects, or uncertainty in the bulk reference.

The short molecular-dynamics production trajectories also introduce temporal sampling uncertainty. Where required, this should be distinguished 
from between-configuration variability. Longer trajectories or additional independent configurations may be necessary if the reported 
statistics are sensitive to the sampling duration or the choice of random seed.

\subsection{Reproducibility and validation}

The workflow retains generated structures, JSON metadata, LAMMPS input files, potential files, manifests, logs, bulk reference 
summaries, and time-averaged output data. The orchestration scripts identify pending jobs, perform preflight validation before 
execution, and create a \texttt{COMPLETE} marker after a simulation exits successfully. These mechanisms improve traceability 
and reduce the risk of inadvertently overwriting or rerunning completed calculations.

Successful execution and completion markers establish operational status rather than physical validity. Before interpreting 
the surface-energy results, the atom-type mapping, realised compositions, bulk reference consistency, slab geometry, and 
numerical convergence must be verified. Convergence with respect to slab thickness and vacuum separation should be 
established independently of the production matrix. The stability of the bulk and slab trajectories and the sensitivity 
of the reported means to the sampling procedure should also be assessed.

Finally, the assumption of an FCC parent structure requires separate justification for each alloy composition and temperature. 
The structure-generation scripts construct FCC configurations by design; they do not establish that FCC is the thermodynamically 
stable phase throughout the composition--temperature range. The calculated values should therefore be interpreted as predictions 
for the specified FCC models and MEAM parameterisation, with the limitations of the potential and the finite sampling protocol 
explicitly acknowledged.
