\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Background and motivation}
A surface is an energetic discontinuity in a crystalline solid. Creating a surface changes the local coordination of atoms and therefore incurs an energy cost. Surface energy affects the relative stability of crystal facets, equilibrium particle shapes, wetting, and other surface-dominated processes. In alloys, local chemical environments also matter: different elements can occupy surface and subsurface sites, and independently generated arrangements at the same overall composition can have different energies \cite{sun2013slabs,yalcin2024surfflow}.

\subsection{Objectives and scope}
This project studies surface energies in the Al--Fe--Ni alloy system using LAMMPS and a modified embedded-atom method (MEAM) potential. The production workflow contains three compositions: equiatomic FeNiAl, Fe$_{20}$Ni$_{50}$Al$_{30}$, and Fe$_{50}$Ni$_{30}$Al$_{20}$. The group study considers the (111), (100), and (110) orientations; the individual production task focuses on (110). The assignment also calls for calculations at 300, 500, and 700~K and three independent chemically disordered configurations per condition. Before interpreting production results, slab dimensions and separation between periodic surface images must be justified by convergence tests. This report covers the theory, potential source and limitations, methodology, and the preliminary convergence dataset currently available. Production results are not presented as completed findings.
