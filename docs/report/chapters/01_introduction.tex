\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Background and motivation}

Surfaces play an important role in determining the energetic and structural 
behaviour of crystalline materials. Creating a surface interrupts the periodic 
coordination of atoms in the bulk, producing under-coordinated environments and 
an associated excess energy. This excess, quantified by the surface energy, 
influences the relative stability of crystallographic facets, equilibrium particle 
morphologies, wetting behaviour, and other processes governed by surface energetics. 
Reliable surface-energy calculations therefore require an accurate description of 
both the bulk reference state and the atomic environments introduced by the surface.

Surface energy depends on crystallographic orientation because different crystallographic 
planes expose different atomic arrangements, surface densities, and coordination 
environments. For a given crystal structure, close-packed and more open surfaces can 
exhibit different energetic costs, although their relative stability must be established 
for the material and conditions under investigation rather than assumed universally. 
Systematic construction of surface slabs and convergence testing are consequently 
important when comparing the energetics of different orientations 
\cite{sun2013slabs,yalcin2024surfflow}.

In multicomponent alloys, surface energetics are further complicated by chemical disorder. 
Different elements can occupy surface and subsurface sites, and the local chemical environments 
at a surface need not reproduce the nominal bulk composition. Consequently, two configurations 
with identical overall compositions and crystallographic orientations can exhibit different 
surface energies because of differences in their atomic arrangements. This configurational 
variability must be considered when estimating representative surface energies for chemically 
disordered alloys.

Temperature introduces an additional consideration by changing the distribution of accessible 
atomic configurations and their associated energies. Molecular-dynamics simulations can be used 
to investigate the temperature dependence of potential-energy-based surface-energy estimates. 
However, such estimates are not equivalent to thermodynamic surface free energies unless the 
relevant entropic contributions and thermodynamic reference states are also accounted for. The 
interpretation of temperature-dependent results must therefore reflect the quantities calculated 
and the limitations of the interatomic potential employed.

\subsection{Objectives and scope}

This project investigates the surface energetics of chemically disordered Al--Fe--Ni alloys using 
molecular-dynamics simulations in LAMMPS and a modified embedded-atom method (MEAM) interatomic 
potential. Three nominal compositions are considered: equiatomic FeNiAl, Fe$_{20}$Ni$_{50}$Al$_{30}$, 
and Fe$_{50}$Ni$_{30}$Al$_{20}$. The calculations use FCC-based structural models; the thermodynamic 
stability of the FCC phase across the full composition and temperature range is not assumed solely 
from the structure-generation procedure.

The group-wide study considers the FCC (111), (100), and (110) surface orientations, while the individual 
production task focuses on the (110) surface. Calculations are planned at 300, 500, and 700~K, with three 
independently generated chemically disordered configurations for each composition, orientation, and 
temperature. This design enables comparisons of orientation- and composition-dependent energetics and 
provides an initial assessment of configurational variability.

The reliability of these comparisons depends on appropriate bulk reference energies, consistent 
chemical-species assignments, and convergence of the slab geometry. The study therefore includes 
preliminary tests of slab thickness and vacuum separation before applying the selected modelling 
choices to the production calculations. The MEAM parameterisation must also be assessed in light of 
its documented scope and limitations, particularly when interpreting temperature-dependent predictions.

This report documents the theoretical basis of the surface-energy calculation, the interatomic potential 
and its limitations, the computational methodology, and the preliminary convergence results available at 
the time of writing. The preliminary tests are distinguished from the production calculations, and no production 
results or final trends are claimed before the corresponding simulations and validation checks have been completed.
